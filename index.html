📚 AEZ Information

Bangladesh has 30 Agro-Ecological Zones (AEZs), divided into 88 sub-regions and 535 agro-ecological units. AEZ classification considers factors such as physiography, soil characteristics, land type/flooding and agro-climatology.

The information on this page is based on official sources from the Bangladesh Agricultural Research Council (BARC) and Bangladesh Agro-Meteorological Information Service (BAMIS).

📋 Five Main Steps of IPM

A working IPM plan is usually built around five repeating steps that guide a farmer from spotting a problem to solving it with minimum risk.

  • 1. Monitor & Identify — regularly inspect the field and correctly identify the pest, since control decisions depend on knowing exactly what is causing the damage.
  • 2. Set an Action Threshold — decide the pest level at which action actually becomes worthwhile; not every pest sighting needs treatment.
  • 3. Prevention — the first line of defence: resistant varieties, crop rotation, timely sowing and field sanitation to stop pests before they start.
  • 4. Choose a Control Method — combine cultural, mechanical, biological and, if truly needed, chemical methods, starting with the least risky option.
  • 5. Evaluate — check whether the action worked and feed that result back into the next round of monitoring.

🌾 Cultural Control

Cultural control means adjusting normal farming practices so the field becomes a less comfortable place for pests to begin with. It is largely preventive and one of the cheapest tools available.

  • Crop rotation and intercropping to break a pest's life cycle
  • Choosing resistant or tolerant varieties suited to the local area
  • Timely sowing/transplanting so the crop avoids the pest's peak season
  • Balanced fertilizer and irrigation, since over-fertilized or waterlogged fields attract more pests and disease
  • Removing and destroying infected plant residue after harvest

🐞 Biological Control

Biological control uses a pest's own natural enemies — predators, parasitoids and disease-causing microorganisms — to keep its numbers down without chemicals.

  • Predators such as ladybird beetles, spiders and dragonflies that feed directly on pest insects
  • Parasitoid wasps that lay eggs inside pest larvae and destroy them from within
  • Microbial agents like Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt), used against caterpillar pests including stem borer and leaf folder
  • Protecting these natural enemies by avoiding broad-spectrum pesticide sprays whenever possible

🖐️ Mechanical & Physical Control

Mechanical and physical methods remove or block pests directly, usually once numbers have already crossed a noticeable level.

  • Hand-picking and destroying egg masses or caterpillars
  • Light traps and pheromone traps to catch and monitor adult moths
  • Yellow sticky traps for whiteflies, aphids and other sap-sucking insects
  • Physical barriers or nets to keep flying pests away from seedbeds

🌿 Botanical / Biopesticide

Botanical pesticides are made from plant extracts that carry natural pest-repelling or pest-killing compounds, offering a middle ground between cultural methods and synthetic chemicals.

  • Neem-based extracts, one of the most widely used botanical pesticides against a broad range of insects
  • Biopesticides derived from bacteria, fungi or viruses that specifically target certain pests
  • Generally lower toxicity to humans and beneficial insects compared with synthetic chemical sprays
  • Best used as part of a rotation, not as the sole control method, to avoid pests building tolerance

🧪 Chemical Control

Chemical control is kept as the last resort in IPM — used only when monitoring shows the pest has crossed the action threshold and other methods aren't enough on their own.

  • Select a pesticide registered for the specific crop and pest, and follow the recommended dose exactly
  • Prefer targeted, selective products over broad-spectrum sprays to spare beneficial insects
  • Rotate chemical groups so the pest does not build up resistance over successive seasons
  • Always follow label safety instructions and pre-harvest interval before selling or eating the produce
